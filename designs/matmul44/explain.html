Before a synthesizer turns Verilog into gates, the idea is school arithmetic: every output cell is one row dotted with one column. This page animates that idea, then walks the real matmul44.v the same way the hardware does it — with AND, OR, and XOR. No clocks. No flip-flops.

2-bit elements (0–3)
6-bit results (max 36)
1,408 mapped cells

Why these widths

2 bits in, 6 bits out

Largest element is 3. Four products, each at most 3×3 = 9. Their sum is at most 36, which fits in 6 bits. The chip never waits on a clock: every multiply and add is just wires and gates.

That is why the ports are packed buses, not sixteen separate pins per matrix — next section.

Act 2

How 16 numbers become one bus

The chip does not have 16 pins for A. It has one 32-bit wire a[31:0]. Each matrix cell owns two bits. Click Play and watch that slice light up on the bus.

Act 3

Inside one C[0][0]

The generate loop instantiates this datapath sixteen times. Play it for the demo’s first output cell.

mul2

2×2 array multiply. Partial products are ANDs; half-adders are XOR + AND. Product 0…9 in 4 bits.

add_rc bit i

Ripple carry. Propagate p = x⊕y, generate g = x∧y, sum = p⊕cin, carry = g ∨ (p ∧ cin).
